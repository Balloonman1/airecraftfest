What is a balloon glow?

Hot air balloons that stay on the ground and light up the night

Pilots inflate full-size hot air balloons after dark and fire their burners so each envelope glows like a giant lantern. The balloons stay tethered and grounded while the crowd walks the field, meets the pilots and takes photos up close.

The glow is produced by Seattle Ballooning, the largest hot air balloon company in the Pacific Northwest. Their Seattle Glow drew 25,000 people to Seattle Center on a Sunday night two years in a row.

The night

How the night unfolds

Come early, grab dinner from the trucks and find your spot before the sun goes down.

  1. 7:00

    Gates open

    DJ on the field, food trucks, beer and wine garden, vendor row and the kid zone.

  2. 8:00

    Balloons arrive

    Crews roll out and lay out the envelopes. Watch the balloons come to life with cold-air inflation.

  3. 9:00

    The Glow

    Burners fire in time with the music and the field lights up. Walk among the balloons and meet the pilots.

  4. 10:30

    Grand finale

    All balloons fire together for the closing count, then the field winds down.

Plan your night

Getting to the glow

The glow is part of Aire Craft Festival in Downtown Issaquah, about 30 minutes east of Seattle and 15 minutes from Bellevue.

Getting here

  • By car: I-90 East to Exit 17 toward East Sunset Way.
  • By bus: Route 554 from Downtown Seattle and Route 271 from Bellevue. Issaquah Transit Center is a short walk.
  • By bike: Free valet bike parking on SE Bush Street, courtesy of Cascade Bicycle Club.
  • Parking: Paid festival lots at Issaquah Middle and High School, Clark Elementary and the King County Library, plus street parking downtown.

What to bring

  • Your ticket on your phone or printed.
  • A blanket or low chair for the lawn.
  • Layers. Issaquah evenings cool off fast after sunset.
  • A charged phone. The glow is one of the most photographed nights of the year.
  • ID if you plan to visit the beer and wine garden or VIP.

FAQ

Good to know

Is the festival free? Why is the glow ticketed?

Aire Craft Festival is free all four days. Balloon Glow Night is a separate evening event with its own field, balloons, pilots, crews and propane, so it is ticketed.

Do the balloons fly?

No. At a glow the balloons stay on the ground and tethered. Pilots fire the burners to light each balloon from the inside, so you can walk right up to them.

What happens if it is windy or raining?

Balloon pilots make every call based on safety. If wind or rain limits inflation, balloons may glow in smaller numbers or as burner displays, and the music, vendors and kid zone continue. All tickets are non-refundable.

Do kids need a ticket?

Kids under 5 are free. Kids 5 to 17 are half price. Adults are 18 and up.

Can I bring my dog?

Please leave pets at home. Burners are loud and the field is crowded. Service animals are welcome.

Can I bring outside food or drinks?

Sealed water bottles are welcome. Please support the food trucks and vendors for everything else. No outside alcohol.

Who produces the glow?

Balloon Glow Night is produced by Seattle Ballooning in partnership with Aire Craft Festival, co-produced by Culture Tug and Clown Garage.

Does the glow support a local cause?

Yes. Balloon Glow Night is a fundraiser for the Issaquah Middle School PTSA, supporting STEM programs for Issaquah students. The PTSA also sponsors the Aire Craft Sky Parade.
